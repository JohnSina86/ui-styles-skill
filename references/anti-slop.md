# Anti-slop tells

Named tells of a default AI-generated page: what each one is, why it reads as generic, and the fix in this skill's own vocabulary (tokens, styles, composition moves, guardrails). Run it on every page you deliver, after the composition moves and before the section 7 checklist.

**Scope.** These are prevalence observations: patterns that appear on a large share of default AI-generated sites. They are not detection methods. None of them proves a page was generated, and none proves it is bad; a deliberate page can carry one for a reason. That is why every tell has an applicability exception tied to the brief or to a token block's documented use, and why a count needs the evidence named in its row. **Sources:** the hyper.ai summary of Adrian Krebs's analysis that scored 15 patterns across 500 Show HN landing pages (hero badge with Inter, gradient backgrounds with glass cards, coloured left borders, icon-topped feature grids; its manual check put false positives at 5 to 10%, which is the reason for the exceptions), the dev.to piece "Why do AI-generated websites all look the same", Search Engine Journal's "7 common AI website mistakes that are easy to avoid", and tells seen in this skill's own outputs. Summaries, not research.

**Rubric v1** (2026-10-13). The table is frozen. A benchmark that scores a page records this version, and any change to a row, its severity, exception, evidence rule or counting unit is a new version with a new number.

## How to count
- **Severity.** `high`: on its own it makes the page read as a template, or it asserts something untrue (logos, quotes or numbers the brief never supplied). `medium`: it adds to the template look, and one instance with a stated reason is tolerable.
- **Applicability.** A tell is not counted when its *Not a tell when* condition holds and you name that condition in the delivery note: the brief line or the token block it rests on. An exception you can't point at is not an exception.
- **Evidence.** Count a tell only when the *Evidence* in its row is visible in the source or in the render. "Looks like it" without that evidence is a note, not a count. Say which you used. Source is always available; "render" only after a browser render in this session (section 7 checklist).
- **Unit.** *Pattern* tells count once per page, however many sections show them. *Element* tells count once per occurrence: each grid, each gradient, each logo row. A page is one route or view, so report multi-page work page by page.
- **Where the counts go.** Tells are craft findings. Report them under their own heading in the delivery note, never as a `ux-laws` law row or score. The laws grade structure and behaviour, and a tell is neither.

## Could this be any site?
Answer each question in one sentence with its evidence before delivery, under their own heading in the delivery note. A question you can't answer with evidence gets "no evidence", not a guess.
1. **Copy.** Cover the brand name and logo. Does the headline still say what this is and who it is for? Name the words that do it.
2. **First screen.** Name the one huge thing and the one thing in the accent on the first screen. If you can't name one of each, or there are two of either, say so.
3. **The thing itself.** Where on the page is the actual product, place or person (a real screenshot, the brief's photo, the real menu)? Name the section.
4. **Shape.** Scrolled at 1440 with the text unreadable, which two sections differ in shape from the rest, and how?
5. **Style.** Which indexed style is this, and which three choices (type pairing, surface shape, backdrop or imagery) came from its token block rather than a framework default?

## Checks
Add these to the section 7 checklist for every page you deliver.
- [ ] Every rubric row checked against the source, and against a render if one was made this session. Counts reported per page by id and severity, with the unit used.
- [ ] Zero high tells. At most two medium tells per page, each named with the reason it stays.
- [ ] Every exception claimed names the brief line or the token block it rests on.
- [ ] The five questions answered, one sentence of evidence each, in the delivery note.
- [ ] Tells and answers sit under their own heading, never in a `ux-laws` table or score.
- [ ] "Checked in render" only after a browser render in this session; otherwise "source only", with the render-only evidence left open.
- [ ] The rubric version is named: anti-slop rubric v1.
